    <title>Long Story Short 2023: Join Kickstarter’s Annual Celebration of Short Films in March</title>
    <content type="html">&lt;h1 id="h:Launch-a-short-film-campaign-and-join-a-cohort-of-boundary-pushing-rabble-rousing-future-dreaming-filmmakers-doing-the-same-" class="page-anchor"&gt;Launch a short film campaign and join a cohort of boundary-pushing, rabble-rousing, future-dreaming filmmakers doing the same.&lt;/h1&gt;
&lt;div class="template asset" contenteditable="false" data-alt-text="" data-caption="" data-id="38738896"&gt;
&lt;figure class="image"&gt;
&lt;img alt="" class="fit" src="https://i.kickstarter.com/assets/038/738/896/2a05ad2bc2d20e7b92a889c9d2d7a0c0_original.jpg?fit=scale-down&amp;amp;origin=ugc&amp;amp;q=92&amp;amp;v=1664412356&amp;amp;width=700&amp;amp;sig=UBJseDzs%2FOXrm7OXdyr33rz5qUJQkPhdY%2BQH0toTklg%3D"&gt;
&lt;/figure&gt;

&lt;/div&gt;


&lt;p&gt;There’s no better place to launch a short film than on Kickstarter. And there’s no better time for shorts on Kickstarter than in spring. &lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;That’s because March 2023 sees the return of &lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/short-films" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;Long Story Short&lt;/a&gt;, Kickstarter’s annual celebration of short films and the visionary people who make them. Throughout the month, we’ll feature live short film projects from every genre—on the site, on social, and in newsletters. We’ll share new resources to help creators run successful campaigns, announce fresh opportunities from industry partners, and showcase some of the outstanding filmmaking and filmmakers we’ve worked with over the years.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;Since the launch of the first Long Story Short in 2020 we’ve seen 400 projects go through our yearly open call, generating more than $4M in pledges. These films join more than 8,000 short films that have been successfully funded on the platform since our launch 13 years ago. &lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;Short films have always found success here. Shorts funded on Kickstarter have been featured at film festivals (catch &lt;i&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/tryhardfilm/pram-snatcher-a-short-film" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;Pram Snatcher&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/i&gt; at this year’s BFI London Film Festival!), released on DVD and Blu-Ray (like Don Hertzfeldt’s &lt;i&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/worldoftomorrow/world-of-tomorrow-the-first-three-episodes-on-blu-ray/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;World of Tomorrow&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/i&gt;), and selected as Vimeo Staff Picks (check out Matthew Puccini’s &lt;i&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/1755952613/lavender-lgbtq-short-film/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;Lavender&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/i&gt;). What’s more, Kickstarter-funded shorts have picked up three Academy Awards: &lt;i&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/matthewacherry/hair-love-animated-short-film" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;Hair Love&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/i&gt; won in the Animated Short Film category in 2020, &lt;i&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/1552995776/the-pad-project" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;Period. End of Sentence&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/i&gt; won in the 2019 Short Documentary category, and &lt;i&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/1131717127/inocente-homeless-creative-unstoppable" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;Inocente&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/i&gt; took home the award in the Short Documentary category in 2013.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;h1 id="h:An-open-call-for-creators" class="page-anchor"&gt;An open call for creators&lt;/h1&gt;&lt;p&gt;It’s never too early to start planning for Long Story Short. So, we’re inviting emerging and established filmmakers, film students, and other enthusiastic cinephiles to start thinking now about what they want to launch into the world.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Here’s how you can get involved with Long Story Short:&lt;/b&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;ul&gt;
&lt;li&gt;
Create a short film project on Kickstarter and select “Shorts” as your subcategory.&lt;/li&gt;
&lt;li&gt;Launch your project anytime between March 1 and 31, 2023.&lt;/li&gt;
&lt;li&gt;
&lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/creators/film" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;Read our guides&lt;/a&gt; for tips and best practices for running a campaign. &lt;/li&gt;
&lt;/ul&gt;&lt;p&gt;Between now and March 2023, we’ll share resources for short-form filmmakers and announce fresh opportunities from industry partners. Then, in March, we’ll promote shorts in Kickstarter newsletters, throughout the site, on social media, and more.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;We’re excited to see what you come up with!

    <title>Kickstarter Never Tolerates Abusive Behavior; This New Feature Makes it Easier for Creators to Take Action</title>
    <content type="html">&lt;div class="template asset" contenteditable="false" data-alt-text="" data-caption="" data-id="38285410"&gt;
&lt;figure class="image"&gt;
&lt;img alt="" class="fit" src="https://i.kickstarter.com/assets/038/285/410/ce83131a43ddf7d8557c3ec3d6f5e876_original.jpg?fit=scale-down&amp;amp;origin=ugc&amp;amp;q=92&amp;amp;v=1660594732&amp;amp;width=700&amp;amp;sig=w9ows2kF5sb5yCA0HtpOZMPBlb1S8FWTzRlQrY6RclE%3D"&gt;
&lt;/figure&gt;

&lt;/div&gt;

&lt;p&gt;Hello, Kickstarter Community. My name is Elana, and I’m Kickstarter’s VP of Product and Design. Today I’m writing to tell you about a new feature we’ve launched that builds on other recent improvements we’ve made to the platform as part of our commitment to keep creators and backers safe.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;Creators will sometimes report to us that they’ve experienced harassment in the comments on their project page or in their project updates. Because comments on a project can only be left by backers of that project, we’ve seen that there are sometimes people who become backers (often making very small pledges) only so they are able to leave abusive comments. Obviously, these are not backers who are pledging to support a project or its creator in good faith, and creators (as well as other backers) have let us know that they would like us to do something about it.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;Our solution is a new feature that enables creators to flag potentially abusive backers to our Trust &amp;amp; Safety team for review. Using this feature, a creator can quickly and easily let us know about a backer they believe is harassing them. Comments from this backer will be immediately hidden from public view while our team reviews the creator’s request and determines whether the backer has indeed violated our &lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/help/community" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;Community Guidelines&lt;/a&gt; and should be permanently removed.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;It’s important to know that flagging a backer through this request does not guarantee that they will be removed from a project. Instead, it alerts us to a situation that a creator believes we should look into, and enables us to quickly act on it. There are of course times when a backer might leave a legitimate critical comment that a creator doesn’t like—comments that do not violate our guidelines will not cause a backer to be removed from a project.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;To submit a request, a creator will need to select the comment that they believe to be abusive and click on the backer’s name. They’ll see a “Remove backer” button in the modal that opens up which they can click to start the request. The creator will then need to write a description (at least 140 characters) of why they believe the backer should be removed as well as select the appropriate category that aligns with their explanation. This information will help give us the context we need in order to investigate and make a decision.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;If our Trust &amp;amp; Safety team agrees that the backer has violated our guidelines, we’ll email the creator a confirmation and remove the backer from the project. We’ll also send the backer an email alerting them to the fact that they have been removed from the project and are no longer able to back it—the backer will also see this information in a banner at the top of Kickstarter’s site when they are logged in.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;If our team decides that the backer didn’t violate our guidelines, the creator will receive an email with this update, and the backer’s comments will be made visible once again. In cases like this, we will not notify the backer about the request—they will only be alerted if we ultimately determine that they have violated our guidelines.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;I want to send a big thank you to all the creators and backers who have generously given their time to talk to us behind the scenes about new features like this, and who have provided valuable insight into what they’d like to see from us. On that note, if you haven’t already &lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/blog/reporting-back-from-the-first-meeting-of-the-kickstarter-comm" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;read up&lt;/a&gt; on the work we’re doing with the &lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/articles/kickstarter-community-advisory-council" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;Kickstarter Community Advisory Council&lt;/a&gt;, please do! This &lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/blog/welcome-to-the-members-of-kickstarters-community-advisory-cou" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;amazing group&lt;/a&gt;, which launched in June, is made up of a diverse group of Kickstarter creators from across a wide range of creative industries. We’re thrilled to be collaborating with them to make Kickstarter as useful, welcoming, and inclusive as it can possibly be.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;i&gt;—Elana Jacobs; VP, Product and Design&lt;/i&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Read about other safety improvements we’ve recently made to Kickstarter:&lt;/b&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/blog/improving-comment-moderationpart-of-our-commitment-to-keeping-0" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;Improving Comment Moderation—Part of Our Commitment to Keeping Creators and Backers Safe&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/blog/more-commenting-improvements-designed-to-keep-creators-and-ba" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;More Commenting Improvements Designed to Keep Creators and Backers Safe

&lt;p&gt;Earlier this month, I was honored to host the inaugural meeting of Kickstarter’s Community Advisory Council with &lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/blog/welcome-to-the-members-of-kickstarters-community-advisory-cou" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;members&lt;/a&gt; Ivan Askwith, Annette Azan, Ellia Bisker, Stefanie Black, Kat Calamia, Jose Cardona, Thomas Negovan, Tony Patrick, Philip Reed, George Rohac, Philomena Schwab, and Philip Winter. I’m happy to let you know about two updates we’ve launched to make Kickstarter better for both creators and backers.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;h1 id="h:Pinned-Comments" class="page-anchor"&gt;&lt;b&gt;Pinned Comments&lt;/b&gt;&lt;/h1&gt;&lt;p&gt;First is the ability to pin comments. Up until now, creators were able to pin their project update posts to the top of their project page, but they didn’t have the ability to pin comments. This meant that if lots of backers had the same question about something and posted about it in a project’s comments, creators would often find themselves replying with the same answers over and over, instead of being able to simply pin a question (along with its answer) to the top of the page so that everyone could easily refer to the information.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;Creators have let us know that this is a feature they would enthusiastically take advantage of, and so we’ve added it. Creators can now pin (and unpin) comments. They can pin up to five comments, as long as they aren't replies, in both the project's comment section and within a project update. We hope this change will help creators communicate more easily with backers, and will help backers get the info they need without having to dig around for it.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;div class="template asset" contenteditable="false" data-id="37954625"&gt;

&lt;h1 id="h:Alt-Text" class="page-anchor"&gt;&lt;b&gt;Alt Text&lt;/b&gt;&lt;/h1&gt;&lt;p&gt;Next is the ability to add alternative text (or “alt text”) to images. Alt text is a word or short phrase that describes an image. One important way it is used is to increase accessibility—for example, for visually impaired people who rely on screen readers to navigate the web. With this update, creators have the option to include alt text to project descriptions and project updates when adding new images or editing existing images.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;We highly recommend that creators add alt text to their images. You can do this by clicking the pencil icon in the top right corner of the image when you're in the editor. The best way to approach adding alt text is to keep it concise and specific (e.g., “a small brown dog sitting on a purple chair”).&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;Before releasing this feature widely, we were fortunate to be able to test it out with Richard Rieman, the creator of &lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/richardrieman/the-winnie-the-pooh-project" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;The Winnie-the-Pooh Project&lt;/a&gt;, a publishing project that makes Winnie-the-Pooh accessible to blind, deaf, and learning-challenged children. A big thanks to Richard for working with us to make this improvement a reality for everyone on Kickstarter.&lt;/p&gt;

&lt;h1 id="h:Check-This-Project-Out" class="page-anchor"&gt;&lt;b&gt;Check This Project Out&lt;/b&gt;&lt;/h1&gt;&lt;p&gt;Before I go, I’d like to point you to a Kickstarter project that I think is especially cool, and that I hope you’ll take some time to check out. Julia Greenberg’s &lt;i&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/jgdorydoc/dory-previn-on-my-way-to-where" target="_blank" rel="noopener"&gt;Dory Previn: On My Way to Where&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/i&gt; is a documentary about the singer-songwriter and lyricist Dory Previn, whose music is brilliant and whose life story is wild and inspiring. Greenberg and her friend/collaborator Dianna Dilworth were able to access Previn’s archives and promise to tell her story in the careful and complex way it deserves. Give it a look and consider backing it!&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;i&gt;—Ellie Clinton, Senior Project Manager&lt;/i&gt;&lt;/p&gt;</content>

We feel very fortunate to have them work with us.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;Our first meeting with the Council will take place at the end of this month. At that meeting, we’ll focus on getting product feedback from the Council to help us orient our roadmap for feature development. I’m really looking forward to digging in with the Council members to hear what they have to say and exploring their ideas for making Kickstarter the best it can be.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;Please join me in welcoming the members of the Kickstarter Community Advisory Council, and read a bit about each of them below. &lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;===&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Ivan Askwith&lt;/b&gt; is a strategist and producer who specializes in organizing fan communities to reach their shared goals, and working with creators and causes to create more meaningful fan relationships. She is following in the footsteps of other changemakers that she has worked for—including Diane Benson, Norma Kamali, and Donna Karan—by leading women down a new path that encourages them to reclaim and embrace their bodies.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Ellia Bisker&lt;/b&gt; is a songwriter, singer, and performing artist who has released ten albums since 2007 with her bands Charming Disaster, Funkrust Brass Band, and Sweet Soubrette. She holds a Master’s degree in Arts Administration from Columbia University and has over a decade of experience in nonprofit fundraising and development. She is an ASCAP Plus Award recipient in recognition of her songwriting, a Brooklyn Arts Council grantee, and a two-time alum of Crosshatch Center for Art &amp;amp; Ecology’s artist residency program. She has run several successful Kickstarter campaigns of her own and has managed or consulted on a number of crowdfunding projects for other creators.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Stefanie Black&lt;/b&gt; is a Queer creator (They/Them) celebrating their tenth year of running “Creepy Kawaii,” a line of professionally made plush dolls, tarot, and enamel pins. They have successfully completed over a dozen Kickstarter projects during their artistic career, and have sold their products to lovers of the strange and unusual worldwide. When Black is not working on their art for Creepy Kawaii or traveling to artistic events, they are working on creating a retro-style horror RPG video game called &lt;i&gt;Casting Shadows&lt;/i&gt;, which they plan to launch on Kickstarter in the future.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Kat Calamia&lt;/b&gt; is the co-writer and co-creator of &lt;i&gt;WEBTOON&lt;/i&gt;, &lt;i&gt;Slice of Life&lt;/i&gt;, and the editor/creator of &lt;i&gt;Bi Visibility: A Bisexual Comic Book Anthology&lt;/i&gt;. She has been a comic book critic and journalist for over a decade, with weekly reviews on her YouTube channel, &lt;i&gt;Comic Uno&lt;/i&gt;, and bylines in &lt;i&gt;Newsarama&lt;/i&gt;, &lt;i&gt;IGN&lt;/i&gt;, DC Comics, &lt;i&gt;Fandom&lt;/i&gt;, and &lt;i&gt;TV Guide&lt;/i&gt;.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Jose Cardona&lt;/b&gt; is a Mechanical Engineer from Puerto Rico and the founder of Mechanical Design Labs. He has led the hardware design for several spacecraft that have successfully launched to the International Space Station, and more recently invented the Author Clock, a “novel timepiece” that uses thousands of quotes from books to tell the time.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Thomas Negovan&lt;/b&gt; is an author, gallery director, musician, and filmmaker based in Los Angeles and Chicago—and the founder of the Century Guild art gallery and museum. He has given a TEDx Talk on the preservation of archaic technologies, and released a vinyl record that was recorded entirely without electricity. Negovan has created more than 60 successful Kickstarter campaigns in publishing, art, and music.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Tony Patrick&lt;/b&gt; is a worldbuilder, immersive director, and founder of the Tenfold Gaming Initiative and the (Re)Writers Room. As a co-author of &lt;i&gt;Batman and the Signal&lt;/i&gt;, a Hereborn Park collaborator, a Guild of Future Architects member, and a Sundance Institute World-Building Fellow, Patrick sculpts spaces that produce artworks, prototypes, frameworks, and civic solutions.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Philip Reed&lt;/b&gt; is a game designer, toy and art collector, blogger, and creator of random junk who spends his days as Chief Executive Officer at Steve Jackson Games. In what free time he can spare, he creates everything from RPG supplements to music. He likes cookies.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;George Rohac&lt;/b&gt; has been a creator advocate for nearly two decades, from helping friends with zines in high school to leading massive projects such as &lt;i&gt;Check, Please!&lt;/i&gt;, &lt;i&gt;Ghostbusters: The Board Game&lt;/i&gt;, and the &lt;i&gt;Bee and PuppyCat&lt;/i&gt; animated series. The goal of their company, Organized Havoc, is to improve the quality of life for creators of every field and focus. They've done this through developing and freely sharing templates and planners for the creator community at large while also managing nearly 100 crowdfunding campaigns through fulfillment.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Philomena Schwab&lt;/b&gt; is a game designer and community manager from Zurich, Switzerland who wrote her master thesis on “Community Building for Indie Developers” and co-founded the indie game studio Stray Fawn. In 2017 Philomena was named a &lt;i&gt;Forbes&lt;/i&gt; 30 Under 30 honoree.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;b&gt;Philip Winter&lt;/b&gt; is the cofounder of Nebia, a startup developing award-winning designs for water-saving showerheads and bathroom products that has raised over $9 million on Kickstarter across five campaigns. Winter has a background in economic development and sustainability projects, and is passionate about employing thoughtful design-centered solutions to the world’s big problems.
